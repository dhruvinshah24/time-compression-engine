# Phase 4 Results — Real Footage Validation
## Time Compression Engine v1.0.1-accuracy-overhaul
## Date: 2026-08-26

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Phase 4 ran structured experiments on the TCE pipeline using synthetic validation
videos (since real surveillance footage was not available locally). All results
are based on **measured observations**, not theoretical claims.

**What was actually tested:** Frame extraction, quality classification, adaptive
frame skip, ROI geometry, model FPS, CLAHE preprocessing.

**What could NOT be tested:** Detection precision/recall/F1 on real people.
All such capabilities remain classified as NOT YET VALIDATED.

**Key discoveries:**
1. YOLO11x and YOLO11l run at **identical FPS** (21.9 vs 21.7) — previously assumed different.
2. The quality classifier classifies **all synthetic frames as UNUSABLE** regardless of brightness — the blur score on flat synthetic frames triggers the UNUSABLE threshold.
3. Adaptive frame skip selects **fewer critical frames** than fixed-5 on the fast-motion test (16.3% vs 20.3% burst retention).
4. ROI entry detection fails when a track starts inside a zone — **a real bug** confirmed by 2 independent tests.
5. YOLO11m detected 23 "tv" objects in a synthetic video with no TV — **false positives on shapes**.

## 5. Model Comparison (PROVEN — FPS only)

**Measured 2026-08-26 on RTX 5050, 100 frames at 1080p.**

| Model | FPS | ms/frame | VRAM Load (MB) | VRAM Inference (MB) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| yolo11n.pt | **38.1** | 26.2 | 30 | 90 |
| yolo11s.pt | 36.7 | 27.2 | 82 | 150 |
| yolo11m.pt | 30.1 | 33.3 | 130 | 268 |
| yolo11l.pt | 21.7 | 46.2 | 234 | 366 |

**Surprise:** YOLO11l and YOLO11x run at virtually identical FPS (21.7 vs 21.9).

**Surprise:** YOLO11m produced 23 false-positive "tv" detections on synthetic
video. YOLO11n, l, x produced 0. This is not a quality measure — it reflects
which models trigger on rectangular shapes that pattern-match COCO classes.

**Detection accuracy (precision/recall/F1): NOT MEASURED.** No annotated real footage.

## 6. Adaptive Skip Comparison (PARTIALLY VALIDATED)

**Measured on fast_motion_60s.mp4 (60s, 25fps, 1500 frames).**
Motion burst: frames 500–750 (t=20s–30s).

| Strategy | Frames Selected | Ratio | Selection Time | Burst Retention |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| fixed_skip_1 (all frames) | 1500 | 100% | 5ms | 100.0% |
| fixed_skip_5 | 300 | 20.0% | 4ms | 20.3% |
| adaptive_mog2 | 174 | **11.6%** | 14,866ms | **16.3%** |

**Critical finding:** Adaptive skip retained **fewer burst frames** (16.3%) than
fixed-5 (20.3%) despite the motion burst. Analysis:

- Adaptive selection overhead: **14.9 seconds** to analyse a 60s video — that's
  a 25% analysis overhead before extraction even begins.
- The MOG2 protection window was triggered but not sustained long enough.
- On synthetic pixel motion (moving rectangle), MOG2 warmup and protection
  threshold calibration may not match real surveillance motion.

**Classification: PARTIALLY VALIDATED.**
The adaptive skip infrastructure works correctly (unit tested). The tuning is not
yet validated on real content. The protection threshold (0.08) may be too high
for the synthetic motion pattern.

## 7. Low-Light Results (UNEXPECTED FAILURE)

**Measured on 4 synthetic illumination levels.**

| Video | Expected | Mean Brightness | Classified UNUSABLE |
|---|---|---|---|
| level_a_normal | NORMAL | 78.8/255 | **100%** |
| level_b_low | LOW_LIGHT | 33.5/255 | **100%** |
| level_c_very_low | VERY_LOW_LIGHT | 10.8/255 | **100%** |
| level_d_unusable | UNUSABLE | 2.8/255 | **100%** |

**All frames classified as UNUSABLE regardless of brightness.**

Root cause: The quality classifier includes a blur score (Laplacian variance).
Synthetic solid-color frames with no texture have near-zero Laplacian variance,
which triggers the `unusable_blur_score` threshold (default: 15.0) on every frame.

This is the classifier correctly identifying that the frames have no useful
texture — the issue is that the test inputs are not representative of real footage.

**Important implication:** The quality classifier cannot be validated using
synthetic solid-color videos. Real footage is required.

**CLAHE effect:** 0.0% brightness gain on all levels — because the classifier
marks them all UNUSABLE first and CLAHE is not applied to UNUSABLE frames.

**Classification: NOT YET VALIDATED.** Cannot test quality classification on
synthetic input that the classifier correctly identifies as textureless.

## 10. ROI Results (REAL BUG FOUND)

**Geometry tests: 3/4 passed.**

| Test | Result | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Point inside zone generates entry | **FAIL** | Track starting inside zone gets 0 events |
| Point outside zone — no event | PASS | Correct |
| Entry then exit — 2 events | PASS | entry + exit both emitted |
| Approach without crossing — no event | PASS | Correct |

**Bug confirmed:** When a track's first observation is inside a zone, no entry
event is generated. The state machine requires a transition from OUTSIDE → INSIDE.
A track that begins inside the zone is never registered as having entered.

**Corpus video simulation:**
- Entry: **NOT DETECTED** (0 events) — related to the above bug
- Exit: detected at t=18.36s (expected 25.7s — error of **7.34s**)
- The exit event is triggered inconsistently

**Classification: PARTIALLY VALIDATED.** Entry/exit detection works in the general
case (test 3 passes). The cold-start bug is real and measurable.

## 15. Unexpected Findings

> "WHAT SURPRISED US"

All observations in this section are directly supported by experiment results:

1. **YOLO11l ≈ YOLO11x in FPS.** At 21.7 vs 21.9 FPS, there is no meaningful
   speed advantage to using yolo11l over yolo11x. The VRAM cost is 366 vs 536MB.
   The accuracy cost of downgrading from yolo11x to yolo11l is unknown (no GT data).

2. **The quality classifier always fires UNUSABLE on synthetic video.** The blur
   threshold catches textureless frames that happen to be correct (they really
   are useless for detection). This is not a bug — it's the classifier doing its
   job. But it means quality classification cannot be validated on synthetic input.

3. **Adaptive skip is slower than fixed skip on 60s video.** The 14.9s MOG2
   streaming overhead is significant for short videos. For a 60s video, this adds
   25% to total processing time before extraction even starts.

4. **ROI has a cold-start bug.** A track that begins its trajectory inside a
   restricted zone never triggers an entry event. This is a real, measurable
   failure that affects any scenario where the camera starts recording while
   a person is already in a restricted area.

5. **YOLO11n is unexpectedly competitive.** At 38.1 FPS (vs 21.9 for YOLO11x),
   YOLO11n is 1.74× faster with a 6× VRAM reduction (90MB vs 536MB). Without
   precision/recall data, we cannot say whether accuracy justifies using the
   larger model.

## 18. Final Answers

1. **Does TCE detect people reliably?**
   INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE. YOLO models are wired. On synthetic video, 0 people
   detected (expected). Needs real footage with annotation.

2. **Does it work in low light?**
   INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE. CLAHE preprocessing is implemented. The quality
   classifier works on real footage (validated Phase 2 at 65ms/frame). But
   detection accuracy improvement from preprocessing cannot be measured on
   synthetic flat-color frames.

3. **Does adaptive skipping preserve important events?**
   PARTIALLY VALIDATED — with a concern. On the fast-motion test, adaptive
   retained fewer burst frames (16.3%) than fixed-5 (20.3%). The protection
   mechanism exists but the threshold may need tuning for real content.

4. **Does tracking preserve identities?**
   INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE. No real-person tracks in Phase 4.

5. **Does ROI crossing work?**
   PARTIALLY — entry/exit detection works in the normal case (PROVEN in test 3).
   Cold-start bug confirmed (FAIL-002). Real footage needed for false-positive rate.

6. **Does crawling detection work?**
   IMPLEMENTED (wired as of Phase 4). NOT YET VALIDATED — no real tracks.

7. **Does the preview show genuine evidence?**
   PROVEN (Phase 2). Thumbnail 15,887 bytes, clip 664,306 bytes, all API endpoints 200.

8. **Which model is actually best?**
   INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE. FPS is measured (yolo11n fastest). No accuracy data.

9. **What is the real processing speed?**
   On synthetic 60s video: extraction + adaptive skip + quality ≈ 36s (0.6× real time).
   YOLO detection adds ~2.7 min for the same 60s video at 22 FPS. RTF with detection ≈ 3×.

10. **Can the architecture scale toward 24h footage?**
    PARTIALLY VALIDATED for pre-detection stages (linear scaling observed).
    Full 24h pipeline (with YOLO) would take ~3× real time = ~72h on current hardware.
    Not yet run. Chunked extraction exists for long videos.

11. **What failed?**
    ROI cold-start bug, adaptive skip under-sampling during bursts, quality
    classifier unusable on synthetic input, YOLO11m false positives on shapes.

12. **What surprised us?**
    YOLO11l ≈ YOLO11x FPS; adaptive skip overhead is significant for short videos;
    quality classifier correctly rejects synthetic frames; ROI cold-start is a
    real gap confirmed by two independent tests.
